The larva swims toward your light. Pass the seven light gates, reach the settlement shelf, and claim a free reef cell before rival larvae take them.

The brain

The larva runs the 237-cell matrix from Ryan et al. 2016: 3,010 chemical connections, 866 directed gap-junction entries and 28 inhibitory cells. Each tick applies brain model v1 from bench/sources/brain.move to every cell: the same integer leaky integrate-and-fire update, with stable gap-junction coupling, an inhibitory reversal floor and spike-frequency adaptation.

PR-I photoreceptors receive your light, shaded by where the ocellus faces. Antenna cells read body tilt. PR-II cells read dimming. The larva swims in bouts while its motor neurons fire, and brighter light brings more frequent bouts. Beat strength follows the motor output, weighted by the neuromuscular junctions in the matrix, and the left/right imbalance bends the tail.

Steering toward the light uses a demo assist gated by real PR-I spikes, and the beat rhythm inside a bout is drawn by the game; the tuned behavioral model is still a design item (DESIGN §9.3). Ticks run at 18 per second here; on-chain the design is one tick per transaction. Gas on the localnet bench (model v1): about 1,640–3,200 computation units per typical tick. The state hash is a BLAKE2b-256 chain over tick, inputs and spikes.
