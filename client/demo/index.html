The larva turns toward your light while its photoreceptors fire, and swims in bouts. Pass the seven light gates, reach the settlement shelf, and claim a free reef cell once the larva has 1,200 ticks, before rival larvae take the cells.

The brain

The larva runs the reconciled 224-cell connectome in research/connectome.v1.json, built from Ryan et al. 2016: 3,010 chemical connections, 428 gap junctions and 28 inhibitory cells. Every tick is stepLarva from client/src/engine.js, the integer mirror of contracts/brain/sources/brain.move that the larva verifier also uses. The genome sets each cell group's threshold and leak, the sensory gains and a left/right motor bias. The body integrator turns spikes into heading, tilt, thrust, escape swims and yolk burn.

Each tick takes what a swim_tick transaction carries: the light's position as unsigned integers, its level (0 to 256), a shadow flag and a pulse flag. PR-I photoreceptors read the light, shaded by where the ocellus faces, and turn the larva toward it while they fire. A passing shadow drives the PR-II cells, and enough PR-II spikes start an escape swim.

Simulated here: the genome (drawn in the browser in place of sui::random), the reef cells, rivals, gates and the shadow's path. The 20-minute competence clock is skipped, and nothing is written to a chain. Ticks run at 20 per second; on-chain each tick is one transaction. The larva's depth, roll and tail beat are drawn over the integer pose, at 80 engine units to the millimetre. The pulse flag only costs yolk in brain.move, so the demo never sets it.

Wiring data: Ryan K, Lu Z, Meinertzhagen IA (2016) eLife 5:e16962, CC BY 4.0. Sign assignments after Kourakis et al. 2019 and Bostwick et al. 2020.
