# Ocellus: design document

> **A reef of sea-squirt tadpoles whose real, fully mapped 177-neuron brains run entirely on Sui.**
> Every neuron updates on every tick, inside Move, and anyone can replay it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Design stage. No contracts deployed. |
| **Chain** | Sui (Move), with Walrus for blob storage |
| **Creature** | Larva of the sea squirt *Ciona intestinalis* |
| **Brain data** | Ryan, Lu & Meinertzhagen (2016), *eLife* 5:e16962, CC BY 4.0 |
| **Last updated** | 2026-10-07 |

The name comes from the **ocellus**, the larva's light-sensing eyespot. The ocellus is the sensor that drives the game's core loop: larvae race toward (or away from) light.

Figures marked **(measured)** come from our own prototype runs. Figures marked **(estimate)** are design assumptions that still need to be confirmed. Everything else is cited.

---

## 1. Pitch and design goals

Ocellus is an on-chain life simulation. Each creature is a *Ciona* tadpole larva with its own genome and a living copy of the real larval connectome. The larva swims using its actual light sensors (ocellus photoreceptors) and gravity sensors (otolith antenna cells). It competes for a spot on a shared reef, settles, and metamorphoses into a sessile adult. The adult then spawns the next generation, and its genes are mixed with Sui's native randomness.

Design goals, in priority order:

1. **Fully verifiable brain.** The whole nervous system steps on-chain every tick. No "brain hash" stands in for off-chain compute, and no neurons are skipped.
2. **Honest data provenance.** Only upstream, commercially usable (CC BY) datasets are used, with credit. The game takes no code or data from other projects.
3. **Launchpad-agnostic token.** The game works with any standard Sui `Coin<T>` and never needs the coin's `TreasuryCap`.
4. **Explicit trust model.** The upgrade policy, admin powers and their hard limits are published on day one. Nothing is called "sealed" unless it is immutable on-chain.
5. **Ship the game first.** A playable hatch-and-swim demo is live at or before token launch.

### 1.1 Lessons from oBrain / Flybook (Arc)

Ocellus was inspired by oBrain's fruit-fly brain and its Flybook society. Our review of their public repository found weaknesses that this design avoids on purpose:

| oBrain / Flybook observation | Ocellus response |
|---|---|
| The fly connectome is pruned hard (each neuron keeps only its strongest few outputs). A "thought" touches only a small fraction of the network. | All 177 CNS neurons (plus their mapped PNS inputs) update **every tick**, over the full synapse list from the source matrix. |
| Founders are seeded from block data that the block producer can influence. | All randomness comes from `sui::random` (threshold-generated by validators), behind non-public `entry` functions. |
| The hub contract can be upgraded by an admin while the project describes it as sealed. | A published upgrade policy (§11). Brain and sink modules are made immutable. |
| Accuracy and benchmark claims can't be reproduced from the repo, and some contracts are unpublished. | All contracts, data-conversion scripts and gas benchmarks are public and reproducible. |
| Code is AGPL and data is CC BY-NC-SA. | No oBrain code or data is used. Ocellus builds from the CC BY primary dataset. |

## 2. Why *Ciona*

- **Small enough to run on-chain in full.** The larval CNS has **177 neurons**. They form **6,618 synapses**, including **1,772 neuromuscular junctions**, plus **1,206 gap junctions** ([Ryan et al. 2016](https://elifesciences.org/articles/16962)). That is the second complete nervous-system connectome of any animal, after *C. elegans*.
- **Open for commercial use.** The paper and its source-data spreadsheets are published under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Attribution is required. Non-commercial terms do not apply.
- **Real, documented behaviors that map to game mechanics.**
  - *Phototaxis*: Type I photoreceptors are mostly glutamatergic and drive direction-sensitive swimming relative to light. Type II photoreceptors are GABAergic and drive a dimming/escape response through a disinhibitory circuit ([Kourakis et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499539/)).
  - *Gravitaxis*: The otolith's excitatory antenna cells feed inhibitory antenna relay neurons. These project asymmetrically to the left and right motor units and produce curved re-orientation swims. Photoreceptor relay neurons gate this circuit, so it switches on when the light dims ([Bostwick et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7066595/)).
- **A natural lifecycle climax.** After a swimming period the larva attaches by its adhesive papillae and resorbs its tail, then becomes a filter-feeding juvenile. One staging study at 20 °C reports a mean of about 9.9 h after hatching before tail absorption begins ([Hotta et al. 2020, *Sci Rep*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-73544-9)). Larvae need about 3–4 h to become competent to metamorphose (same source, citing Matsunobu et al. 2015). Metamorphosis is the game's big on-chain moment.
- **Built-in genetics hook.** *Ciona* is a hermaphrodite with genetically controlled **self-sterility** ([Harada et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152488)). The mechanism uses three multi-allelic gene pairs ([Sawada et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59147-4)). That gives us a biologically grounded mating-compatibility rule (§5.3).
- **Sea-themed.** It fits a reef world and Sui's water branding.

## 3. The brain

### 3.1 Data source and conversion

| Source file (eLife *Figure 16*) | Content | Our count from the file |
|---|---|---|
| `elife-16962-fig16-data1.xlsx` | Chemical synapse matrix (pre × post). Each value is the cumulative presynaptic contact depth in µm. Includes pooled muscle columns and basal lamina. | **3,010** non-zero neuron→neuron entries; 13 entries to muscle (L/R dorsal+medial pools) |
| `elife-16962-fig16-data2.xlsx` | Putative gap-junction matrix (contact depth > 0.12 µm) | **451** non-zero entries (866 directed after symmetrization) |
| `elife-16962-fig1-data1.xlsx` | Cell-type key (class, count, IDs) | 40+ cell classes |

Notes:

- These counts come from our own parsing script (§9). They are **matrix entries (neuron pairs)**, not individual synapses, so they differ from the paper's synapse totals.
- Both matrices together label **237** cells. That total includes PNS sensory neurons (e.g. RTEN/ATEN/BTN) and some labels that differ between the two files (e.g. `Cor1` vs `coronet1`, `BPN` vs `BPIN`, an `Ep` ependymal row). **A reconciliation table is the first data task.** It maps every label to one canonical cell ID and documents each decision.
- **Conversion to weights:** contact depth ÷ 0.06 µm (the section thickness implied by the matrix values, all multiples of 0.06) gives an integer count of 60-nm sections. That count is the synaptic weight. Maximum observed: 277 **(measured)**.
- **Sign (excitatory/inhibitory):** the connectome is structural only, so signs come from neurotransmitter studies and follow Dale's law (one sign per presynaptic neuron). These are seeded from published identities: PR-II photoreceptors and pr-AMG relay neurons GABAergic (Kourakis 2019), antenna relay neurons inhibitory (Bostwick 2020), ACINs inhibitory (their name; cell key in Ryan 2016). Everything else defaults to excitatory, and each default is labeled as one in the published sign table. The sign table is versioned data, separate from the wiring.

The canonical artifacts are:

1. `connectome.v1.json`: cells, edges, weights, signs and provenance for every value.
2. A compact binary encoding (CSR, below).
3. A **Walrus blob** that contains (1) and (2) plus the conversion script. Its blob ID and a `blake2b256` hash of the binary encoding are recorded in the on-chain `Connectome` object ([Walrus docs](https://docs.wal.app/)).

### 3.2 On-chain encoding

The connectome is stored once, as a **frozen (immutable) object**. It is never rewritten, and every creature reads it by reference. Frozen also matters for gas: in our benchmark, an *owned* connectome passed by reference was rewritten on every transaction (≈110 M MIST storage cost churned per tick, almost all of it rebated). Freezing removed that **(measured)**.

```move
/// One per brain model version. Frozen at publish time: never mutated, never deleted.
public struct Connectome has key {
    id: UID,
    version: u16,             // brain-model version
    n: u16,                   // number of cells (canonical, after reconciliation)
    // Chemical synapses, CSR by presynaptic neuron
    row_ptr: vector<u16>,     // len n + 1
    col: vector<u8>,          // postsynaptic index (n <= 255)
    w: vector<u16>,           // weight in 60-nm sections
    inhibitory: vector<bool>, // per presynaptic neuron (Dale's law)
    // Gap junctions, symmetric, CSR
    gap_ptr: vector<u16>,
    gap_col: vector<u8>,
    gap_w: vector<u16>,
    // Role maps: indices of sensory and motor cells
    pr1: vector<u8>, pr2: vector<u8>, antenna: vector<u8>,
    mn_left: vector<u8>, mn_right: vector<u8>,
    walrus_blob_id: vector<u8>,
    data_hash: vector<u8>,    // blake2b256 of the canonical binary encoding
}
```

Size: about 3,010 × 3 B + 866 × 3 B + pointers ≈ **13 KB (estimate)**. Sui's object size limit is **256,000 bytes** (`max_move_object_size`), and a vector may hold up to **262,144 elements** ([protocol config snapshot](https://github.com/MystenLabs/sui/blob/main/crates/sui-protocol-config/src/snapshots/sui_protocol_config__test__Mainnet_version_57.snap); [Move Book: Building against limits](https://move-book.com/guides/building-against-limits/)). We are about 20× below the cap. A single pure transaction argument is limited to 16 KB, so the connectome is created from several vector arguments, which is what the prototype does.

### 3.3 Neuron model: fixed-point leaky integrate-and-fire

Move has no floating point, so the model is integer-only and fully deterministic:

- The membrane potential is a `u32` with a fixed offset (rest = 2²⁰), so no signed integers are needed.
- On every tick, for **every** cell *i*:
  1. **Leak:** `v += (rest − v) >> leak_shift[i]`
  2. **Chemical input:** for each presynaptic cell that spiked on the previous tick, add `±w × gain` to its targets (CSR walk).
  3. **Gap junctions:** a current proportional to the voltage difference with each coupled neighbor.
  4. **Sensory drive:** injected into the photoreceptor or antenna indices (§3.4).
  5. **Threshold:** if `v ≥ θ[i]`, the cell spikes, `v ← reset`, and the spike is recorded.
- Per-cell parameters (`leak_shift`, `θ`, `gain`) come from the **genome** (§5). The wiring comes from the connectome and is identical in every creature. So individuals differ the way real ones do (excitability, sensitivity, side bias), not by rewiring.
- The model is a game abstraction. Real *Ciona* neurons are not all spiking cells, so we will not claim biological accuracy. We publish the behavioral tests the model has to pass (§9.3).

### 3.4 Sensors and motor output

| Game stimulus | Real cells driven | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light intensity / direction | PR-I photoreceptors (23 cells, `pr1–pr23`) | Direction-sensitive. Drive is shaded by the larva's heading relative to the light source (the ocellus pigment cup sits on the right side; Ryan 2016). |
| Sudden dimming | PR-II photoreceptors (7 cells, `pra–prg`) | GABAergic, disinhibitory "escape" pathway (Kourakis 2019). |
| Gravity / orientation | Antenna cells Ant1, Ant2 (otolith) | Drive depends on body tilt. Gated by the light state (Bostwick 2020). |
| Touch / obstacle (later) | Rostral & apical trunk epidermal neurons (RTEN/ATEN) | PNS mechanosensors, present in the matrix. |

**Motor readout:** the motor ganglion has five pairs of motor neurons, MN1–MN5 (Ryan 2016). Each tick the game sums left and right MN spikes, weighted by their neuromuscular-junction weights from the matrix's muscle columns:

- `thrust = L + R`, `turn = R − L`. Tail beats are asymmetric, which produces the helical/curved swims described in the literature.
- Heading and position are integer state on the larva, updated by `thrust` and `turn`, plus reef currents (§7).

### 3.5 Gas and compute: prototype benchmark (measured)

We wrote an unoptimized benchmark of the model above in Move. It uses the **real** 237-cell matrix (3,010 chemical + 866 directed gap entries, 28 inhibitory cells, 25 sensory inputs). We ran it on an isolated **Sui localnet** (CLI `sui 1.80.1`, reference gas price 1,000 MIST/unit), with a frozen connectome and an owned brain:

| Transaction | Computation units | ≈ SUI at 1,000 MIST/unit |
|---|---|---|
| No-op call (same objects) | 1,000 (minimum bucket) | 0.0010 |
| **1 tick, typical activity** | **1,450 – 2,610** | **0.0015 – 0.0026** |
| 1 tick, worst case (every cell spikes) | 12,500 – 12,600 | 0.0125 |
| 2 ticks, typical | 18,100 – 21,100 | ≈0.02 |
| 5 ticks, typical | 210,100 | ≈0.21 |
| 50 ticks, typical | 3,076,000 | ≈3.1 |
| 100 ticks | failed: `InsufficientGas` at the 50 SUI budget cap | |

Storage for the brain write was about 11.4 M MIST, 99% rebated. The non-refundable part was about 0.11 M MIST per tick **(measured)**.

**Key finding: multi-tick transactions get expensive fast.** Sui charges instructions in tiers: about 1× up to 20,000 instructions, 2× to 50,000, 10× to 100,000, 50× to 200,000, 100× beyond that, and 1000× past 10 M ([`gas_model/tables.rs`, `initial_cost_schedule_v5`](https://github.com/MystenLabs/sui/blob/main/crates/sui-types/src/gas_model/tables.rs)). Computation is also capped at 5,000,000 units per transaction ([Sui gas docs](https://docs.sui.io/concepts/tokenomics/gas-in-sui)). Design consequences:

1. **One tick per transaction** for the default action. One "stimulus" from the player is one brain tick and one swim bout.
2. **Optimize to fit inside the first tier.** Planned: drop the per-tick `tabulate` allocations, pack state into `u64` words, keep spike lists sparse, and precompute per-neuron gain. Target: **≥ 4 ticks under ~20k instructions (estimate, to be benchmarked)**.
3. **Bound the worst case.** Genome ranges are clamped so a seizure (all cells firing) is rare and self-limiting. Refractory periods are part of the model.
4. **Mainnet numbers must be re-measured.** The localnet gas price is not mainnet's. Multiply units by the current reference gas price.

## 4. Creature object model

```move
/// The playable creature. key + store: tradable, kiosk-compatible.
public struct Ciona has key, store {
    id: UID,
    generation: u32,
    stage: u8,                 // 0 Egg, 1 Larva, 2 Settling, 3 Adult, 4 Fossil
    genome: Genome,
    brain: BrainState,         // larval brain (cleared at metamorphosis)
    body: Body,
    born_ms: u64,              // sui::clock timestamps
    stage_since_ms: u64,
    parents: vector<ID>,       // empty for founders
    connectome: ID,            // which frozen Connectome version it runs
    // Optional extras (race history, cosmetics) live in dynamic fields
}

public struct Genome has store, copy, drop {
    bytes: vector<u8>,         // fixed-length, e.g. 64 B = 8×8 grid of loci
    sterility_alleles: vector<u16>, // self-sterility loci (§5.3)
}

public struct BrainState has store, drop {
    v: vector<u32>,            // membrane potential per cell
    spiked: vector<u64>,       // bitset of last tick's spikes
    refractory: vector<u8>,
    tick: u64,
    spikes_total: u64,
    state_hash: vector<u8>,    // rolling hash over (tick, inputs, spikes)
}

public struct Body has store, drop {
    x: u32, y: u32, depth: u32, heading: u16, tilt: u16,
    energy: u32,               // yolk reserve: larvae don't feed
}
```

- **Owned, not shared.** Each larva is an owned object, so stepping its brain uses Sui's owned-object fast path without consensus contention. Only reef interactions touch shared objects.
- **Size:** about 237 × 4 B (`v`) + 32 B bitset + 237 B refractory + 64 B genome ≈ **1.3 KB (estimate)**, far below the 256 KB limit.
- **Dynamic fields** ([docs](https://docs.sui.io/concepts/dynamic-fields)) hold optional, growing data (race history, cosmetic items), so the core object stays small and fixed-size.
- **Rolling `state_hash`:** each tick folds `(tick, sensor inputs, spike bitset)` into a `blake2b256` chain. Off-chain replays (§9) can be checked against it in O(1).

## 5. Genome and breeding

### 5.1 Genome layout (64 bytes, an 8×8 grid as in Flybook-style UIs)

| Loci | Controls | Range clamp |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 | Per-class excitability (θ offsets for PR-I, PR-II, antenna, relay, MGIN, MN, …) | ±12% of base |
| 16–23 | Leak time constants per class | 2–5 bit shifts |
| 24–31 | Sensory gain (light, dimming, gravity) | 0.5×–2× |
| 32–35 | Left/right motor bias (helix handedness) | ±10% |
| 36–43 | Yolk reserve, max swim time, competence onset | Bounded by stage timers |
| 44–55 | Cosmetics: tunic color, pigment, pattern | Free |
| 56–63 | Reserved for future brain-model versions | n/a |

The wiring is never mutated. Variation lives in physiology and appearance, so every creature still runs the published connectome, and the verification story stays simple.

### 5.2 Founders

Generation 0 is minted in a non-public `entry fun hatch_founder<T>(…, r: &Random, …)`. All loci are drawn from `RandomGenerator`, then clamped. Following the [Sui randomness guidance](https://docs.sui.io/guides/developer/advanced/randomness-onchain):

- Functions that use randomness are **non-public `entry`**, which blocks composition attacks. Sui also rejects PTB commands that follow a `Random`-using call, except `TransferObjects`/`MergeCoins`.
- `RandomGenerator` is created inside the consuming function and never passed as an argument.
- **Gas parity:** the happy and unhappy paths cost about the same, so nobody can abort selectively on a bad roll. Where outcomes differ a lot (rare traits), we use the two-step **commit/reveal** pattern: step 1 fixes the random value in an object, and step 2 consumes it.

### 5.3 Breeding (spawning)

- Two **adults** on the reef can spawn together when they are within a spawning radius and both off cooldown.
- **Self-sterility rule:** each creature carries *k* self-sterility alleles, after the three gene pairs reported by Sawada et al. 2020. Fertilization fails when the pair shares a matching allele. This blocks selfing, and also clone-to-clone farming, in a biologically grounded way.
- **Crossover:** per-locus uniform crossover from the two parents, then mutation with a small per-locus probability (e.g. 1–2%, **estimate, tunable**), clamped to legal ranges.
- **Clutch:** one spawn event produces *n* eggs (genome-dependent, small). Each egg is a `Ciona` at stage `Egg`.
- **Cost:** spawning costs game coin (§8), and each adult has a cooldown enforced with `sui::clock` ([access on-chain time](https://docs.sui.io/guides/developer/sui-101/access-time)).

## 6. Lifecycle

```
Egg ──hatch──▶ Larva ──(competent + reef spot)──▶ Settling ──▶ Adult ──spawn──▶ Eggs
                 │                                   │            │
                 └── yolk exhausted / timeout ───────┴──▶ Fossil ◀┘ (lifespan end)
```

| Stage | What happens | Clock rules (game time; **estimates**, tunable) |
|---|---|---|
| **Egg** | Created by spawning or founder hatch. Can be traded. | Hatchable after a short incubation |
| **Larva** | Brain is live. Each player action is one brain tick. Swims, races, scouts the reef. Does **not** feed and burns its yolk energy, as real larvae do. | Competence window opens after the minimum swim time. Mirrors the real ~3–4 h competence onset in compressed time. |
| **Settling** | Attaches to a free reef cell (shared Reef write). Settlement is the competitive moment. | Must finish within a deadline or the larva fails |
| **Metamorphosis** | **The on-chain climax.** The tail and its motor circuit are resorbed: the larval `BrainState` is cleared, and a frozen **`LarvalRecord`** keeps the final state hash, total ticks, spikes, best race times and settlement coordinates. The adult gets a reduced "adult nervous system" stub that is not stepped. | One transaction |
| **Adult** | Sessile on its reef cell. Filter-feeds (gains energy from currents passing its cell) and can spawn. | Lifespan in days (estimate) |
| **Fossil** | At end of life the creature becomes a frozen, non-transferable-by-default memorial in the **Tunic Bed** (graveyard). Its genome and lineage stay readable forever. | Permanent |

On "resorbs most of its nervous system": in real metamorphosis the larval tail (notochord, muscle, caudal nerve cord) is absorbed and the adult body plan forms (Hotta et al. 2020). We model this as "the larval brain retires". We keep the claim at that level and don't over-state the neurobiology.

## 7. Reef world and activities

### 7.1 World objects

```move
/// One per reef shard. Shared. Holds the settlement grid and environment.
public struct Reef has key {
    id: UID,
    shard: u16,
    width: u16, height: u16,
    cells: Table<u32, ID>,          // cell index -> settled adult ID
    light_x: u32, light_y: u32,     // current light source
    current_seed: u64,              // drift field, re-rolled by sui::random each epoch-window
    tide_ms: u64,
}

/// Short-lived race. Shared, read-only during the run.
public struct LightRace has key {
    id: UID,
    reef: ID,
    start_ms: u64, end_ms: u64,
    light_path_seed: u64,           // from sui::random at creation (commit), revealed at start
    max_ticks: u32,
    entries: Table<ID, u32>,        // larva -> best distance
}
```

**Sharding:** the reef is split into several shared `Reef` shards. Settlement writes therefore spread across objects, which keeps write contention low. Most activity is owned-object brain ticks.

### 7.2 Activities

1. **Light races.** A race defines a moving light source and a tick budget. Entrants step their own larva with `race_tick(race: &LightRace, larva: &mut Ciona, clock: &Clock)`. The race is read by immutable reference, the larva is owned, and the race's light path determines the sensor input. Each larva can only move under race rules while it is entered. When the window closes, `finalize` records the ranking from each larva's on-chain position. **No off-chain physics:** the race result is the brain's output.
2. **Dimming escapes.** Random "shadow" events (a passing predator) test the PR-II disinhibitory escape circuit. Larvae that dive or turn fast enough keep their energy.
3. **Settlement competition.** Competent larvae race to claim free reef cells. Cells differ in light, current flow and neighbors. Claims are first-come within the transaction order, and a contested cell resolves by who arrived first on-chain.
4. **Currents.** A drift field derived from `current_seed` adds displacement every tick. It is re-rolled periodically with `sui::random`. Larvae that orient well against gravity and light make better headway.
5. **Adult feeding and spawning.** Adults on high-flow cells gain energy faster. Spawning requires a compatible neighbor (§5.3).

### 7.3 Anti-spam / pacing

- Each larva has a per-clock-window tick allowance and a yolk budget, so it can't be ground forever.
- Each tick is a real gas-paying transaction. The brain *is* the cost.

## 8. Token integration (launchpad-agnostic)

### 8.1 Principles

- The game is **generic over any coin type** `T` that follows the Sui coin standard ([docs](https://docs.sui.io/standards/coin)). The type is **bound once** at launch, and the game works the same whichever launchpad minted the coin.
- **Never requires `TreasuryCap<T>`.** Most launchpads lock or destroy minting rights, so the game never mints or burns through the cap.
- **Works before and after bonding-curve graduation.** Players only need to *hold* `Coin<T>` (or an address balance of `T`). Where the coin trades is irrelevant to the contracts.
- **No oracle dependency at launch.** Pre-graduation prices are not reliable oracle inputs, so pricing adjusts with in-game demand (§8.3).

### 8.2 Objects

```move
/// Created once by the deployer; consumed by bind<T>().
public struct BindCap has key { id: UID }

/// Shared game config for coin type T. Exactly one exists.
public struct Game<phantom T> has key {
    id: UID,
    prices: PriceBook,          // per-action dynamic prices (§8.3)
    sink: ID,
    pool: ID,
    bounds: HardBounds,         // immutable after bind
}

/// Locked sink: Balance<T> with NO withdrawal function anywhere in the (immutable) package.
public struct Sink<phantom T> has key { id: UID, locked: Balance<T> }

/// Prize pool for in-game competitions (races, settlement milestones). Not a holder dividend.
public struct PrizePool<phantom T> has key { id: UID, balance: Balance<T> }

public fun bind<T>(cap: BindCap, cfg: InitialConfig, ctx: &mut TxContext) { /* creates Game<T>, Sink<T>, PrizePool<T>; deletes cap */ }
```

- **Binding:** `bind<T>` deletes the `BindCap`, so the coin type is fixed forever. The coin's decimals are read from its on-chain metadata where available, or else supplied once at bind time and emitted in an event for anyone to check.
- **Spending:** every paid action takes a `Coin<T>` and splits it. *x%* goes to the **Sink** (effectively burned: locked with no withdraw path, in an immutable module) and *(100 − x)%* to the **PrizePool**. Default split: **80 / 20 (estimate)**. The split is bounded in `HardBounds`.
- **Prize pool payouts** go only to in-game achievements: race podiums, first settlement on a new reef shard, longest-lived lineage. Payouts follow published rules with per-period caps.

### 8.3 Pricing that survives a 10× price move

Each paid action (hatch founder, extra tick allowance, enter race, spawn) has a price that adjusts with demand, like a fee market:

```
every window W (e.g. 1 hour, estimate):
  if actions_in_window > target:  price *= (1 + step)   // e.g. +12.5%
  if actions_in_window < target:  price *= (1 - step)
  price = clamp(price, min_price, max_price)            // HardBounds
```

- When the token's market price rises, activity drops and in-game prices fall automatically. The reverse also holds.
- **Admin tuning only within hard bounds:** a `PriceAdminCap` can change `target` and `step` within `HardBounds`, which are frozen at bind time. Changes are delayed by a timelock (e.g. 24 h, estimate) and emitted as events.
- **Optional later:** once a deep post-graduation DEX pool exists, the game could reference a TWAP to quote prices in SUI terms. This is an open question (§12), not a launch dependency.

### 8.4 Launch checklist (any launchpad)

1. Game package published, brain and sink modules immutable, demo live on mainnet with SUI-priced or free test actions.
2. Coin launched on the chosen launchpad.
3. `bind<T>` called. Coin type, prices and bounds are emitted in an event and posted publicly.
4. Founder hatching opens.

### 8.5 What we will **not** say

No promises of returns, yields, price appreciation or revenue share to holders. The token is an in-game consumable. The Sink reduces circulating supply only as a side effect of play, and we will describe it neutrally. Legal review comes before launch (§12).

## 9. Verification: anyone can replay

### 9.1 What is verifiable on-chain

- **The wiring:** the `Connectome` is frozen. Its `data_hash` matches the Walrus blob, and the blob contains the conversion script and the original eLife source files' hashes.
- **The model:** the Move source is published and the package ID is verifiable. Brain modules are immutable.
- **Every tick:** inputs (sensor drive from reef and race state) and outputs (spike bitset, position) are on-chain state. Each tick emits a compact event (`tick, spikes_bitset, inputs_digest, state_hash`).

### 9.2 Replay tool

A reference implementation (Rust or TypeScript) of the exact integer arithmetic:

1. Load the connectome from Walrus and check `data_hash`.
2. Read a creature's genome and its tick events from a Sui indexer or RPC.
3. Re-run every tick and compare the `state_hash` chain. Any mismatch is a bug report against us.

### 9.3 Published behavioral tests

These are CI tests on the model, not marketing claims:

- **Phototaxis:** with a directional light, PR-I-driven larvae change heading relative to the light more often than with the light off (qualitative test, after Kourakis 2019).
- **Dimming response:** a step-down in light produces a burst of MN activity through the PR-II pathway.
- **Gravity gating:** antenna-driven turning is suppressed in constant light and released on dimming (after Bostwick 2020).
- **Activity coverage:** a census of the share of cells that fire at least once over N ticks, published so nobody can quietly ship a mostly idle network.

### 9.4 Benchmarks

The gas benchmark package and scripts from §3.5 will be published in the repository in Phase 0, with instructions to reproduce them on localnet or testnet.

## 10. Trading

- `Ciona` has `key + store`, so it works with Sui **Kiosk** and a **TransferPolicy** ([Kiosk docs](https://docs.sui.io/standards/kiosk)).
- **Royalty rule** on secondary sales (e.g. 2–5%, estimate). Royalties flow to the PrizePool or a project treasury, and the split is published.
- Eggs, larvae and adults are all tradable. Settled adults stay on their reef cell. Trading an adult transfers ownership of the creature, and the reef cell stays linked to that creature's ID.
- **Fossils** are non-transferable by default (memorials), though this could be revisited.
- **Onboarding:** **zkLogin** lets new players sign in with an OAuth account and get a Sui address without a seed phrase ([zkLogin docs](https://docs.sui.io/concepts/cryptography/zklogin)). Sponsored transactions can cover the first few ticks.

## 11. Upgrade policy and admin powers

| Module | Policy | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `connectome` (data object) | Frozen object | The wiring can never change. New brain models are *new* frozen objects with a new version. |
| `brain` (LIF math) | **Immutable package** (`sui::package::make_immutable`) once audited | "Verifiable" must mean nobody can change the math. |
| `sink` | Immutable | Proves the locked balance can never be withdrawn. |
| `game`, `reef`, `race`, `breeding` | Upgradeable at first, `only_additive_upgrades` after stabilization, `UpgradeCap` held by multisig with a timelock, eventually immutable | Lets us fix gameplay bugs without touching the brain or the sink. |

Sui policies can only become more restrictive over time ([custom upgrade policies](https://docs.sui.io/develop/publish-upgrade-packages/custom-policies)). We will publish the `UpgradeCap` object ID and its policy level.

**Admin caps:** `PriceAdminCap` (bounded, timelocked) and `ReefAdminCap` (add new reef shards). There is no cap that can move funds out of the Sink, change a creature's genome, or alter the connectome.

## 13. Open questions and risks

| Risk / question | Notes and mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Gas per tick on mainnet** | Measured on localnet at about 1.5–2.6k units per typical tick, unoptimized. Mainnet RGP and protocol versions differ, so re-benchmark on testnet and mainnet. Tiered instruction pricing punishes multi-tick transactions (§3.5). |
| **Worst-case activity** | An all-spiking tick costs about 12.6k units. Use genome clamps, refractory periods and per-tick spike caps. |
| **Object and transaction limits** | Object 256,000 B, vector 262,144 elements, transaction 128 KB, pure argument 16 KB, max 2,048 objects created per transaction ([Move Book](https://move-book.com/guides/building-against-limits/)). The current design is far below all of them. Shared-object congestion on reef shards is handled by sharding. |
| **Data curation** | The two matrices use inconsistent labels, and signs are partly inferred. The reconciliation and sign tables must be published with every decision explained. Treat the model as a game abstraction, not neuroscience. |
| **Biological accuracy claims** | Never claim "a real larva lives on-chain". Claim "the published wiring of a real larva drives this creature, verifiably". |
| **Randomness pitfalls** | Non-public `entry` only, gas parity, commit/reveal for high-variance outcomes (§5.2). |
| **Admin trust** | Immutable brain and sink, bounded and timelocked admin caps, published `UpgradeCap` (§11). |
| **Token / legal** | No return promises. The prize pool pays for gameplay achievements, not holdings. Get legal review of prize mechanics (sweepstakes or gambling rules vary by jurisdiction) and of token marketing before launch. |
| **Regulated coins** | If a launchpad issues a *regulated* coin with a deny list, the issuer could restrict addresses. Document the dependency, and prefer unregulated coins. |
| **Attribution** | CC BY 4.0 requires credit to Ryan, Lu & Meinertzhagen (2016) in the app, the repo and the Walrus blob. |
| **Pricing without oracles** | Demand-based pricing can lag sharp price moves. Hard bounds limit the damage, and TWAP is an optional later addition. |
